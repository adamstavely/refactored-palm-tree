# ADR-002 — AI Output Fingerprinting Strategy

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-05-05
**Deciders:** Platform Architect, Security Lead

## Context

Content generated by AI within the THEMIS platform will be copied out of the platform into external tools (Microsoft Word, email, local document editors) and may return as filed work product or uploaded documents weeks or months later. When content returns, the platform must be able to identify whether it originated from a THEMIS-governed AI interaction. This is the transport tracking problem. The solution must work across realistic transit paths: copy → paste into Word → edit → export to PDF → upload back.

Three candidate techniques exist: Zero-Width Character (ZWC) encoding, homoglyph substitution, and lexical watermarking.

## Decision

Use a **three-signal composite approach**: lexical watermarking as the primary mechanism, ZWC encoding as an opportunistic secondary signal, and registry-based LSH similarity detection as the universal fallback. No single signal is relied upon; all three contribute to a composite confidence score.

**Lexical watermarking** (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) biases token selection during generation according to a secret key — influencing synonym choice, sentence rhythm, and word ordering in ways invisible to human readers but statistically detectable. Survives copy-paste and light editing; degrades gracefully as edit distance increases. Requires controlling the generation process (which the API gateway provides).

**ZWC encoding** injects invisible Unicode characters encoding the chunk_id after the first sentence. Fast to extract; provides a direct chunk_id if the signal survives transit. Fragile — stripped by PDF export, OCR, and most rich text editors. Treated as a bonus, not a baseline.

**LSH registry detection** runs any submitted content against the chunk registry using locality-sensitive hashing for fuzzy matching. Works even when both other signals are lost. The only mechanism that does not require prior embedding in the content.

## Alternatives Considered

### Option A — Homoglyph substitution only
Replaces standard characters with visually identical Unicode equivalents, encoding a payload in the substitution pattern. More robust than ZWC; survives more transit paths. However: modifies the actual character stream, causing indexing and search anomalies. Detectable by opposing counsel with basic tooling. Inappropriate for legal work product where character-level fidelity matters in court filings. Rejected entirely.

### Option B — ZWC encoding only
Simple to implement; provides a direct chunk_id lookup. However: stripped by too many realistic transit paths (PDF export → OCR, email clients that normalize Unicode, rich text editors). A primary mechanism that fails on the most common document workflows provides false confidence. Rejected as primary mechanism; retained as secondary signal.

### Option C — Registry-based detection only (no embedding)
Purely reactive: check submitted content against the registry. No proactive embedding. Simple; no risk of modifying content. However: requires content to be submitted to a detection endpoint — it cannot identify provenance in documents that are never submitted back. Retained as universal fallback; insufficient alone.

## Consequences

**Positive:**
- Three independent signals mean no single point of failure
- Composite confidence score provides probabilistic attribution rather than binary match/no-match
- Lexical watermarking survives the most realistic transit paths while remaining invisible
- ZWC provides fast positive identification when it survives (no registry lookup needed)
- LSH fallback catches content even when both embedded signals are lost

**Negative:**
- Lexical watermarking adds latency to generation (biased sampling at token level); estimated < 50ms overhead
- Requires model provider cooperation or post-generation synonym substitution; the latter is less effective than inference-time watermarking
- Watermark detection requires the secret key; if the key is lost, detection capability is lost for that generation period

**Risks:**
- Determined paraphrase defeats all three signals: an attorney who reads and substantially rewrites AI output leaves no detectable trace. This is by design — substantially rewritten content is genuinely the attorney's work. Policy decisions about disclosure obligations for AI-informed writing are outside the scope of this technical mechanism.
- Key management: watermark keys must be versioned and retained indefinitely (or at least for the retention period of any content they watermark). Mitigation: Vault-based key management with explicit versioning.
